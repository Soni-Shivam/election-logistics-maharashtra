Search robustness

Each road-run profile was searched twice from different random seeds. Close scores mean the result is not luck; a large gap means the search needs more steps or restarts before a plan is trusted.

Limits of these results

Population, not electors

Seats are balanced on 2011 Census population; the booth model assumes 70% are electors. Official seats were drawn on 2001 population, so part of their imbalance is a decade of growth.

City cells and wards

Big cities are cut into ~1 km WorldPop grid cells rescaled to the Census town total (Sangli uses 20 approximate wards). City totals are exact, but how people are spread inside a city is a model estimate. Official city seats then show implausible swings (Pune Cantonment +67%, Kothrud −45%), so treat any seat marked ≈ as an estimate. Census ward populations would fix this.

Official map reconstruction

Villages use SHRUG's village-to-seat key; city cells use the DataMeet seat shapes. The two agree on 98–99% of villages with no measurable offset, so the shapes are sound. A few official seats show a one-village stray piece where key and shapes disagree; that is reconstruction noise, not a real exclave.

One point per village

Each village or cell is one point, so walking inside it counts as zero. Booth and walking figures measure travel between villages only.

Assumed costs

Booth cost ₹25,000, walking ₹10 per elector-km and EVM transport ₹120 per booth-km are placeholders. The booth count depends mainly on the walking limit.

Heuristic search, real seat counts

ReCom finds good plans, not proven-best ones. Seat counts are the official ones (Sangli 8, Satara 8, Nashik 15, Pune 21); a 2011-based statewide reallocation would change some of them.
